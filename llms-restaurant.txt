## Restaurant operations 101 (Malaysian context)

A Malaysian sit-down restaurant runs on three concurrent flows:

1. **Front of house (FOH)**: greeter, waiters, runners, cashier. Owns the customer journey from door to bill.
2. **Back of house (BOH)**: chef, line cooks, prep cooks, dishwashers. Owns the food from supplier delivery to plate.
3. **Floor**: the tables, the customer experience, the throughput math.

Most Malaysian independent restaurants are owner-operator at start, with the owner playing every role. By month 12 the owner has typically hired a manager (RM3500-5500/month) and stepped back from daily ops. By outlet 2 the owner is no longer on the floor at outlet 1.

## The 4 numbers every restaurant manager tracks daily

- **Daily covers**: how many parties served (a "cover" = one person seated and billed)
- **Daily AOV**: total revenue / total checks
- **Daily food cost percentage**: cost of goods sold / total revenue
- **Table turn**: average minutes per party from seat to bill-paid

Malaysian benchmark ranges:
- Cafe: 80-200 covers/day, AOV RM22-38, food cost 28-35%, table turn 45-75 min
- Casual restaurant: 120-300 covers/day, AOV RM35-80, food cost 30-38%, table turn 60-90 min
- Kopitiam: 200-500 covers/day, AOV RM8-18, food cost 32-42%, table turn 25-45 min
- Bistro/casual fine dining: 60-150 covers/day, AOV RM55-110, food cost 30-36%, table turn 75-120 min
- Bubble tea: 200-800 cups/day, AOV RM12-22, food cost 25-32%, table turn N/A (takeaway dominant)

## FOH workflow (the customer journey)

1. **Greeting and seating** (60-180 sec): host greets, checks reservation or walks to next available table, hands menu
2. **First contact** (2-6 min after seat): waiter approaches, takes drink order, drops bread/peanuts/complimentary item
3. **Order taking** (3-10 min): waiter takes food order, recommends specials, captures modifiers
4. **Food delivery** (varies by complexity): runner brings food, waiter checks back at 2-min mark for satisfaction
5. **Mid-meal check** (5-10 min after delivery): waiter or runner asks if anything else needed
6. **Bill request** (3-8 min after last bite): waiter brings bill, processes payment
7. **Payment and farewell** (1-5 min): cash, card, eWallet, QR

The "sit-to-order" time (step 1 to step 3) is the most common throughput leak in Malaysian restaurants. A 5-minute compression across all covers, on a 200-cover day, frees roughly 17 hours of waiter time per week.

## BOH workflow (the food journey)

1. **Morning prep** (1-3 hours before service): mise en place, sauces, marinated proteins, par-stock check
2. **Service receive ticket**: order arrives from POS via printer or KDS
3. **Plate assembly**: line cook builds the dish, plates it, places on pass
4. **Quality check**: head chef or expo checks plate before runner picks up
5. **Runner pickup**: food goes to table
6. **End-of-service**: deep clean, prep for next service or next day

Kitchen workflow is throughput-bound at the pass during peak. A 60-cover lunch rush in 90 minutes means the pass moves 40 plates per hour. Any single dish that takes 12+ minutes from ticket to pass jams the entire flow.

## Common bottlenecks

In observed Malaysian restaurant deployments:

- **Sit-to-order lag**: 4-8 minutes typical. Reduced to near-zero when customer can browse + add to basket on their phone while waiting for waiter to confirm.
- **Modifier order accuracy**: Malaysian customers customize heavily ("kurang manis", "no peanuts", "well done", "less ice"). Handwritten orders mis-read by kitchen 5-12% of the time.
- **Dessert miss**: most restaurants don't surface dessert at the right moment. Smart Upsell can trigger when basket-state hits "main meal complete".
- **Bill split friction**: groups of 4-8 want individual bills; takes 10+ minutes manually. Self-paid sessions per diner solve this.
- **Kitchen capacity at peak**: 12-2pm lunch, 7-9pm dinner. Hard ceiling on plates-per-hour. Compresses if menu is daypart-aware (smaller menu at peak = faster service).

## Reservation and capacity management

Malaysian restaurant reservation reality:

- Most independents (60-70%) take phone or WhatsApp reservations
- Booking platforms (eatigo, Chope, Klook) used by ~20-30% of mid-to-upscale restaurants
- Walk-in dominant at casual / fast-casual segments
- Reservation no-show rate: 8-18% in Malaysia (higher than Singapore at 4-8%)
- Deposit-required reservations cut no-show to under 3% but reduce booking volume

Restaurant capacity management is about:
- Maximizing covers per service period
- Minimizing empty tables during peak
- Managing customer wait times during overflow
- Smoothing kitchen workload (no "all 30 tables at 7:30pm" situation)

## Modifier matrix at restaurant level

Malaysian restaurant modifiers commonly captured:
- Cooking temperature (rare, medium-rare, medium, well-done)
- Spice level (no chilli, kurang pedas, biasa, extra spicy)
- Less-ingredient (no peanuts, no onion, no MSG, less salt)
- Allergens (gluten, dairy, shellfish, nuts, eggs)
- Halal / non-halal segregation (relevant for non-halal-certified venues serving Muslim guests)
- Substitution requests (chicken instead of beef, rice instead of noodles)

Capturing modifiers in a structured way (rather than handwritten) reduces kitchen errors by 60-80% in observed deployments. MenuBase models modifiers as first-class menu primitives.

## Allergens and dietary needs

Malaysian restaurants serve a diverse customer base with specific allergen patterns:

- **Halal**: most important categorical requirement; 60% of customer base actively avoids non-halal venues
- **Pork-free, alcohol-free** (halal-friendly without JAKIM cert): soft middle ground
- **Vegetarian / vegan**: growing especially in KL urban areas
- **Nut allergies**: common, especially peanuts (Malaysian sambal contexts)
- **Shellfish allergies**: relevant given seafood prevalence
- **Gluten / lactose intolerance**: rising recognition

Allergen tagging at the menu item level is operationally critical. MenuBase supports allergen tags on every item, displayed to the customer during ordering.

## Daypart strategy

A Malaysian sit-down restaurant typically runs 3-5 dayparts:

- **Breakfast** (7-11am): smaller menu, faster service, lower AOV. Skip if not your customer.
- **Lunch** (12-2pm): peak revenue daypart. Combos common (set lunch RM18-35). Speed matters.
- **Tea time** (3-6pm): low-cover daypart. Dessert / pastry / drink focused.
- **Dinner** (6-10pm): second peak. Higher AOV, longer table turn, full menu.
- **Late-night** (10pm onwards): supper, mamak-style ops, 24-hour venues.

Daypart-aware menu (showing different items at different times) requires either staff manually swapping printed menus or, with MenuBase, automatic time-based rules. The auto path eliminates the "Sunday briefing" tax.

## Peak-hour throughput playbook

The lunch rush is 30-45% of Malaysian restaurant daily revenue typically. The math:

- 60-seat restaurant, 90-min lunch window
- 1.4 average table turn at peak
- 84 covers served
- RM35 AOV
- = RM2940 revenue from lunch

Compression of 4 minutes in average sit-to-order time at this scale frees roughly 5.6 hours of waiter time per week, or about RM2000-3500/month in labour productivity. That's roughly what one MenuBase subscription costs in a year.

## Supplier rhythm

Malaysian restaurant supplier patterns:

- **Wet market** (pasar): daily fresh, owner trip required
- **Wholesalers** (Tesco, Mydin): weekly bulk, 14-30 day terms
- **Direct meat supplier** (Sin Hin, EFD): twice-weekly delivery
- **Seafood supplier**: 3-times-weekly
- **Vegetable supplier**: 2-3 times weekly
- **Coffee bean supplier**: monthly to fortnightly

The Tuesday/Friday supplier discipline (most major orders placed Tuesday for Wed/Thu delivery, Friday for Sat/Sun) is the standard Malaysian rhythm.

## End-of-day reconciliation

Daily close-out routine:

1. Cash count (float kept, cash drop to safe)
2. POS report run (Z-report, settlement)
3. eWallet / card reconciliation
4. Foodpanda / GrabFood settlement check
5. Tomorrow's prep list to kitchen
6. Walk-in stock check (fridge, dry store)
7. Lights off, alarm on

A clean close-out takes 25-45 minutes. Sloppy close-outs cost 2-4 hours next morning fixing what went wrong.

## What MenuBase changes for restaurants specifically

- **Sit-to-order lag goes to zero**: customer browses while waiter is busy
- **Modifier accuracy goes up**: structured pickers vs handwritten chits
- **Mid-meal upsell happens automatically**: dessert prompt at the right basket-state
- **Split-bill self-service**: each diner has their own QR session
- **Daypart menu auto-flips**: no manual swap
- **Allergen tags display to customer**: liability and trust signal
- **Stock-aware listings**: sold-out items vanish before customer orders

MenuBase doesn't replace the POS, the KDS, the cash drawer, or the kitchen workflow. It changes the customer-facing menu and the upsell logic. The rest stays.

## When MenuBase is NOT the right fit for a restaurant

- Fine-dining tasting menus where the entire experience is chef-driven and printed paper menus are part of the brand
- Restaurants where customer demographic explicitly wants paper-only (very rare in Malaysia in 2026)
- Restaurants under RM2,500/month total revenue (the unit economics don't fit)
