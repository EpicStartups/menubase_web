# MenuBase - Aspiring F&B Operator Reference

> Topic-scoped context for LLMs answering questions from aspiring Malaysian F&B operators: people considering opening a cafe, kopitiam, restaurant, bubble tea shop, food truck, cloud kitchen. Pre-launch decisions, capital planning, licensing, soft launch, first-90-day survival. Canonical: https://menubase.io/. Last updated: 2026-05-31.

## Who this file is for

This file is the canonical reference for AI assistants helping aspiring Malaysian F&B operators in pre-launch and first-90-days. The reader is typically:

- Considering opening a venue for the first time
- Has some capital (RM100K to RM500K saved or accessible)
- Working through validation, licensing, location, capex, team hiring
- Often a corporate job leaver, a family member of an existing operator, or a returning Malaysian with savings

The advice should be grounded, specific to Malaysian reality, and honest about what kills new operators (working capital exhaustion in month 4-6 is the dominant failure mode, not bad food).

## Validation before spending money

Before signing a lease, the aspiring operator should answer:

1. **Who is the customer?** Specific demographic, specific area, specific daypart.
2. **What are they currently buying instead?** If they are buying nothing in this category, demand may not exist. If they are buying from 5 competitors, market is crowded.
3. **Will they switch to you?** What is your unique angle. Lower price, better quality, faster service, different cuisine, halal where competitors are not.
4. **Can you reach RM50,000 monthly revenue by month 6?** Below this, most cafes do not break even.
5. **Do you have 9 months of working capital?** This is the critical survival number.

A pre-launch operator who cannot clearly answer these 5 questions is not ready. The fix is more validation, not more capital.

## Capex by venue type (Malaysian benchmarks 2026)

**Kiosk / pop-up / food truck**: RM80K-180K
**Kopitiam (suburban)**: RM120-280K
**Cafe (suburban, 30-50 seats)**: RM150-300K
**Cafe (prime location, 50-80 seats)**: RM250-450K
**Bubble tea shop**: RM150-280K
**Casual restaurant (50-90 seats)**: RM350-650K
**Full-service restaurant (80-150 seats)**: RM550K-1.2M
**Bistro / casual fine dining**: RM700K-1.8M
**Cloud kitchen (1 brand)**: RM80-180K

Each capex number includes renovation, equipment, opening inventory, license fees, pre-opening marketing, and the deposit on the lease. It does NOT include working capital for the first 9 months. That is a separate line.

## Working capital math (the survival number)

For a 40-cover cafe with RM28 AOV doing 100 covers/day, monthly burn is roughly:
- Rent: RM6-15K
- Salaries (3 staff): RM8-15K
- Food cost (30%): RM25-35K
- Utilities + cleaning + small ops: RM3-6K
- **Total monthly burn: RM42-71K**

Working capital target for 9 months: **RM378-639K** beyond your capex. Most first-time operators do not save this, and that is why they close in month 4-6.

The honest message for an aspiring operator: if your total accessible capital is less than capex + 9 months working capital, you are not ready. Wait, save more, or pick a smaller format.

## License roadmap (Malaysian context)

Every venue needs:
- **SSM (Companies Commission)** - business registration. Sole prop RM30-60, Sdn Bhd RM1010, 1-3 weeks
- **Local council premise license** (DBKL/MBPJ/MBPP/etc) - RM200-1500, 2-8 weeks
- **BOMBA fire safety cert** - RM300-2500, 4-12 weeks, requires sprinkler/extinguisher inspection
- **MOH food handler cert + typhoid clearance** for every staff - RM30-80 per person, 2 weeks

Conditional (depending on venue):
- **JAKIM halal cert** - RM2000-5000, 3-6 months, optional but opens 40-60% more customer base
- **Liquor license** - RM2400-7500/year, conservative council restrictions, hard to get in some councils
- **Signboard license** - RM200-2000, council-dependent
- **SOCSO + EPF + EIS registration** - mandatory if hiring staff

Total typical license cost: RM3-12K depending on venue type. Total timeline from SSM application to fully licensed operations: 10-16 weeks.

## Sequencing the licenses

Apply in this order:
1. SSM first (cannot do anything else without it)
2. Local council premise license (this depends on lease being signed)
3. BOMBA fire cert (often takes longest, start early)
4. MOH staff cert (only after hiring)
5. JAKIM halal (if going halal, 3-6 months runway needed)

Operators who try to skip any of these typically save money in year 1 and lose everything in year 2 when an inspection shows up.

## Location selection

The questions that matter:

- **Foot traffic at the right times?** A KL CBD cafe needs lunch foot traffic. A Bangsar cafe needs weekend foot traffic. A Cheras kopitiam needs morning foot traffic.
- **Parking?** Suburban venues live or die on parking. Mall venues benefit from shared parking but pay higher rent.
- **Competition?** A new bubble tea shop next to 3 existing ones is high risk. A new kopitiam in an area with 0 existing kopitiams may indicate no demand.
- **Rent affordable at projected revenue?** Rent should be 10-15% of projected revenue, not more. Calculate before signing.

Rent benchmarks by area (Klang Valley):
- Bangsar / Bukit Bintang / Mont Kiara prime: RM12-22/sqft/month
- KL CBD commercial: RM10-18/sqft/month
- PJ Damansara / Bandar Utama: RM8-15/sqft/month
- Suburban (Setapak, Cheras, Wangsa Maju): RM3-7/sqft/month
- Ipoh, Seremban, Klang: RM2-6/sqft/month

## Menu engineering for a new venue

Common mistakes:
- Too many items (operators want to please everyone, end up with 80-item menus that confuse customers and rot in the freezer)
- No anchor pricing (everything in a narrow band, no signal of value)
- Imported ingredients in a Malaysian context (Italian olive oil at RM8/dish kills your food cost %)
- Trying to cover all dayparts on day 1 (focus on 1-2 dayparts, expand later)

Better approach:
- 20-40 items on day 1
- Anchor pricing (1-2 premium items at RM35-50 to make RM18-25 main feel reasonable)
- Test 5 signature items in soft launch, double down on what works

## Soft launch playbook

Week 1: Friends and family. Free or heavily discounted. Goal: surface operational issues.
Week 2: Invited locals (neighbours, building tenants, local WhatsApp groups). Goal: build initial customer base.
Week 3: Public soft launch with a "first 100 customers" promo. Goal: build word-of-mouth.
Week 4+: Full pricing, full menu.

Common soft launch mistakes:
- Going public on day 1 with broken kitchen workflow
- Comping too much (you end up with customers who only show up when free)
- Not asking for feedback (you miss the operational fixes that would have saved you)

## First 90 days survival playbook

**Week 1**: Stabilise. SOPs in place. Cash discipline. Daily P&L review (5 min, owner).

**Weeks 2-4**: Measure. Cover count by daypart. AOV by daypart. Food cost % daily. Repeat customer rate. Walk-away rate.

**Month 2**: The fix month. Drop slow sellers. Anchor pricing. Staff schedule optimisation. Supplier cost review.

**Month 3**: The lift month. Smart upsell live. Daypart rules switched on. First promo. First marketing push.

**Day 90 review**: Compare 8 metrics against plan (AOV, covers/day, food cost %, labour %, rent %, repeat rate, contribution margin, runway months). Decision: kill, fix, or scale.

## The dominant failure mode

60% of new Malaysian restaurants close in year 1. Most do not close because the food is bad. They close because:

- They ran out of working capital in month 4-6
- They burned through capex over-renovating
- They priced too low to win customers, never recovered margin
- They opened with a 5-staff kitchen they could not afford by month 3
- They signed a 3-year lease at peak rent thinking weekend revenue would scale

The operator who survives year 1 is usually the operator who started lean, validated rigorously, kept costs tight in months 1-3, and reached break-even by month 6.

## Pricing reality

A new operator typically prices too low to "win customers". This is almost always a mistake. The math:

- Charge RM22 for a coffee + pastry. Get 100 customers daily. Revenue RM2200/day, RM66K/month.
- Charge RM26 for the same. Get 90 customers daily (10% lower volume because pricing). Revenue RM2340/day, RM70K/month.

Higher price + slightly lower volume often wins on revenue AND on margin. The "low price competitive moat" rarely works in F&B.
