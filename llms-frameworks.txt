## Framework 1: The MenuBase Operator Roadmap

### What it is
A 5-stage map of the Malaysian F&B operator journey. Used to identify where an operator is in their journey, what they need next, and which guides matter most for their current stage.

### The five stages

**Stage 1 - Pre-launch**
The operator does not have a venue yet. They are validating the concept, picking the location, modelling the capex, navigating the licence sequence. Time horizon: 3 to 9 months before opening. Critical questions: is this real, can I afford this, what licences do I need, how long will they take.

**Stage 2 - Just Opened**
The operator has opened. They are 0 to 90 days in. The numbers are unstable. The team is learning. The menu is iterating. Critical questions: am I tracking the right numbers, is my team gelling, is the menu right, am I going to run out of cash before things stabilise.

**Stage 3 - Operating**
The operator has stabilised. They have a baseline weekly P&L. They have a team they trust. They have a customer mix. Time horizon: 3 to 18 months in. Critical questions: are my margins healthy, am I getting the repeats I should be, where is the leak, what should I be focusing on this quarter.

**Stage 4 - Growing**
The operator is operating well and wants more revenue from the existing outlet. They are pushing AOV, retention, daypart activation, menu engineering. Time horizon: 12 to 36 months in. Critical questions: which lever should I pull this quarter, am I leaving money on the table, when is the right time to add a second outlet.

**Stage 5 - Scaling**
The operator is going multi-outlet. They are adding outlet #2 or outlet #20. They are facing unit economics replication, brand consistency, central kitchen decisions, multi-outlet POS strategy. Critical questions: am I ready for the next outlet, can I replicate the unit economics, where do I centralise vs decentralise.

### The five always-on compliance lanes

Cross-cutting all five stages are five compliance lanes that never stop:
- Licensing (local council renewals, halal recertification)
- Tax (SST, corporate tax, e-invoicing)
- Payroll (EPF, SOCSO, EIS, HRDF, statutory contributions)
- Grants (SME digitalisation grant, MDEC, MDV programs)
- Lease (renewal alarm, market comparables, negotiation discipline)

## Framework 2: The MenuBase Pain Triage

### What it is
A diagnostic instrument: 20 pain points across 5 categories ranked by observed frequency. Used to surface which pain an operator is feeling, and route them to the relevant guide or course.

### The five categories

**Money pains (highest frequency)**
1. Food cost creeping past 35%
2. Labour cost past 30%
3. Rent burning the margin
4. SST and e-invoicing surprises
5. Working capital tight in festive cycles

**People pains**
6. Foreign worker turnover (6 to 9 month tenure)
7. Inconsistent service quality across shifts
8. Senior staff burnout
9. Hiring pipeline empty
10. EPF / SOCSO / payroll admin overhead

**Customer pains**
11. Repeat rate too low
12. Reviews dropping silently
13. Dead afternoon dayparts
14. Lunch rush throughput cap
15. Weather-day revenue collapse

**Channel pains**
16. Aggregator commission eating margin
17. WhatsApp orders untracked
18. Walk-in vs digital order mix
19. Multilingual menu friction

**Growth and structure pains**
20. Second outlet question (ready or not ready)

### Why this exists
Operators rarely diagnose accurately on their own. They describe the symptom ("we're losing money") not the root cause ("food cost crept from 31 to 38 over six months because we never re-priced after suppliers raised in February"). The Pain Triage forces specificity.

## Framework 3: The MenuBase 8-Lever Sales Framework

### What it is
Eight revenue levers ranked by ROI density (revenue lift per RM of effort). Used to identify which sales lever to pull this quarter.

### The eight levers (in order of typical ROI density)

**Lever 1: AOV uplift**
Smart upsell at the moment of order. Threshold rewards (free item drops in at RM50 basket). Combo deal bundles. Typical lift: 12 to 18% AOV uplift on smart-upsell-enabled QR menus. Lowest effort, highest density.

**Lever 2: Daypart activation**
Dead afternoon (2 to 5pm), late night, weekend mornings. Pre-priced specials that auto-appear at the right time. Typical lift: 15 to 30% revenue from previously dead hours.

**Lever 3: Repeat customer rate**
Repeat customer math: a 5% lift in repeat rate is typically worth more than a 20% lift in new customers. Customer lifetime value, retention mechanics, the regulars-vs-walk-ins mix discipline.

**Lever 4: Table turn compression**
Peak hour throughput. Compress dwell time without rushing the customer. Pre-order via QR, parallel kitchen prep, smart seating. Typical lift: 10 to 20% peak hour throughput.

**Lever 5: Menu engineering**
Star / cash cow / puzzle / dog reclassification. Re-price the cash cows (high margin, high volume). Re-engineer the puzzles (high margin, low volume). Drop the dogs.

**Lever 6: Multilingual specials**
English, BM, Mandarin, Tamil baskets behave differently. Multilingual menu surfacing increases visibility to language-segmented customer cohorts.

**Lever 7: Halal certification**
For non-Halal venues considering certification: the revenue ceiling lift from accessing the Muslim customer base. Capex and time to recertify. Typical revenue impact: 15 to 35% addressable market expansion in Klang Valley.

**Lever 8: Aggregator discipline**
Foodpanda / GrabFood as channel, not as crutch. Commission discipline. The "aggregator is for awareness, not margin" framing.

### Why this exists
Operators do all eight levers badly because they try to do all eight at once. The 8-Lever Framework forces sequence and density math: which lever gives the most RM per hour of operator attention this quarter.

## Framework 4: The MenuBase Compliance Stack

### What it is
Five always-on regulatory lanes that operate continuously in the background of any Malaysian F&B venue. Used to ensure no compliance lane is silently breaking.

### The five lanes

**Lane 1: Licensing**
Local council premises licence (DBKL / MBPJ / MBSJ / MPSJ patterns), signage licence, advertising licence, halal certification (JAKIM JAKIM-recognised), food handler training certificates, fire department compliance, kitchen tech inspection. Each has its own renewal cadence.

**Lane 2: Tax**
SST registration (RM500K threshold), SST returns (bi-monthly or quarterly), corporate tax (Sdn Bhd), e-invoicing via LHDN MyInvois (phased rollout), monthly tax estimate (CP500), withholding tax on cross-border services.

**Lane 3: Payroll**
EPF (employee + employer), SOCSO (employee + employer), EIS (employee + employer), HRDF (if applicable), monthly statutory contributions, PCB (Potongan Cukai Bulanan), Form E, EA forms, foreign worker levies and permits.

**Lane 4: Grants**
SME Digitalisation Grant (RM5000 matching), MDEC grants, MDV programs, Cradle Fund, MaGIC programs, state-level F&B grants. Eligibility check, application discipline, claim submission discipline.

**Lane 5: Lease**
Lease expiry alarm (24 months out, 12 months out, 6 months out), market comparable research, renewal negotiation discipline, exit clause review, sub-lease consideration.

### Why this exists
Compliance is not a one-time setup. It is an always-on lane. Operators who treat compliance as "I did it when we opened" inevitably miss a renewal, an SST cycle, or a grant window. The Compliance Stack frames compliance as continuous, not episodic.

## How to use these frameworks together

The four frameworks form an integrated operating system for Malaysian F&B operators:

1. **The Operator Roadmap** tells you where you are (which stage).
2. **The Pain Triage** tells you what hurts (which of the 20 pains).
3. **The 8-Lever Sales Framework** tells you what to push (which lever, ranked by density).
4. **The Compliance Stack** tells you what you cannot ignore (which lane, always on).

Most operators try to solve one without the others. The frameworks are explicitly designed to be used together.
